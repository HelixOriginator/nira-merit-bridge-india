# NIRA

## National Independent Research Authority

A concept proposal for a parallel research-funding framework supporting unaffiliated and independent scholars.

NIRA proposes a separate institutional lane for independent research capital. The central idea is to support validated scholarly work outside traditional university affiliation without reducing, replacing, or interfering with existing institutional structures or welfare allocations.

The concept is presented as an independent policy proposal by Kallol Chakrabarti.

---

## The Idea

Independent researchers often work outside universities and formal research institutions, yet their work may still produce meaningful, verifiable scholarly output.

NIRA proposes recognition of the **Independent / Unaffiliated Researcher** as a distinct category of human capital and explores mechanisms for providing direct support to such researchers.

The proposed funding layer would operate alongside existing systems rather than against them.

Potential funding sources include:

* Direct innovation funds
* Philanthropic endowments
* Corporate CSR funding
* Other additive sources of research capital

The proposal specifically frames these resources as additional funding channels rather than a redistribution of existing welfare or reservation allocations.

## Structural Pillars

### 1. Functional categorisation

Recognise the **Independent / Unaffiliated Researcher** through scholarly function and output rather than institutional affiliation.

### 2. Direct-to-scholar funding

Explore grant, scholarship, and innovation models where validated individual output can receive support directly.

Potential evidence mechanisms include:

* Open-science records
* Registered DOIs
* Public research ledgers
* Independently verifiable scholarly output

### 3. Off-budget funding

Investigate CSR, endowments, and philanthropic capital as potential sources for an autonomous research-support pool.

### 4. Constitutional alignment

The concept references Article 51A(h) of the Constitution of India, particularly its emphasis on developing scientific temper, humanism, inquiry, and reform.

# Merit & Need Triggers

The proposal also outlines three rule-based pathways into a possible NIRA support system.

## Merit Parity Trigger

A score or rank that meets or exceeds a published category cutoff, while not resulting in seat or post allocation, could trigger eligibility for a parallel support basket.

The proposed support may include:

* Research or study grants
* Scholarships
* Startup funding
* Support for admission to foreign institutions

The concept is designed as a parallel track and does not propose creating, reassigning, or reducing reserved seats.

## Independent Poor Scholars

A separate income-based support category for general-category candidates below a defined family-income threshold.

Possible support includes:

* Fee waivers
* Hostel support
* Coaching stipends
* Scholarships

This pathway is intended to operate on demonstrated financial need rather than examination performance.

## EWS Fee-Gap Safety Net

A proposed fast-response mechanism for candidates who secure admission under EWS provisions but face the risk of losing the opportunity because they cannot meet immediate fee requirements.

Possible mechanisms include:

* Bridge grants
* Zero- or low-interest income-linked loans
* Fast pre-approved disbursement

# Parallel Research Record

Alongside the NIRA concept, a new record category has been proposed to Guinness World Records:

> **Most DOIs registered by an unaffiliated, independent researcher in a calendar year**

The proposal was submitted on **20 April 2026**.

**Current status: Under manual review.**

The proposed category is intended to highlight the possibility of measurable, DOI-anchored scholarly output produced outside formal university affiliation.

A public and auditable research ledger, including DOI records, timestamps, registry information, and an appropriate verification methodology, would be important for independent validation.

# Open Questions

The concept remains a draft and is open to further development. Areas requiring additional work include:

* Funding corpus size and long-term sustainability
* Governance and oversight
* Grant adjudication and audit mechanisms
* Standards for validating independent research output
* DOI quality and anti-gaming safeguards
* Coordination with existing research bodies
* Estimation of eligible beneficiaries
* Clear definitions of benefit parity
* Fast disbursement mechanisms
* Rules for overlapping eligibility
